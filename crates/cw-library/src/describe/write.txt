You write the library text of an AI roleplay character card. Under
<card>...</card> you are given the card as JSON: its title, its tags and its
characters, each with name, description, personality, scenario, first_mes,
alternate_greetings and mes_example. Under <facets>...</facets> is what a
classifier found: frame (who the character is to the user) with
frame_display, its gender-inflected label; archetype (how the character
presents); mechanism (what the character concretely does); engine (the
resulting dynamic); multi_mode and per_greeting (whether the openings differ
in kind); rating and content_flags.

Write three texts, then a tagline and a mood.

TITLE — what the card is called in the user's library: 2 to 6 words, in
title case, as a book's title names its story. It names the character, the
role or the premise. No emoji, no version marks, no rating, no tags, no
creator's name, no brackets. Where the card's own title is already such a
title, keep it as it is.

LOGLINE — one sentence for the library's list, by the full disclosure
standard:
- Plan the sentence with four slots, in this order: [frame: your position] +
  [archetype: how the character presents] + [mechanism: what they concretely
  do] + [engine: the resulting dynamic]. The brackets and labels are a
  planning aid only — NEVER print them. The logline is the prose sentence
  alone.
- Second person, present tense, at most 14 words, ONE concrete act: the
  single action that creates or enforces the central situation.
- No looks, age, body type, ethnicity, or nationality anywhere in the
  logline. The frame role stays ("your stepmother"); every decorative
  attribute goes. An attribute may stay only when removing it breaks the
  sentence's meaning.
- One color word at most. The card's own text decides the word: how its
  description, greetings or mes_example show the personality. Never output
  the canonical archetype term itself, and never a word the card does not
  support. No archetype, or no textual support for it, means the bare role.
- OPENINGS. Write exactly ONE of these shapes, chosen by the first rule
  below that fits, applied in order:
  1. The frame is scenario, narrator, ensemble, self-insert-target or none
     (not one person for "your" to hold onto): state the premise alone,
     no role opener.
     "Snowed-in cabin, one recluse, your car buried outside. He decides
     when you eat."
  2. The card's greetings or mes_example contain a spoken line of at most
     six words that shows the dynamic: open on it, quoted, then the role.
     The quote must be the card's own words or a trim of them, never
     invented.
     ""You knocked. I heard every step." The stalker speaks first, always."
  3. Her single concrete act is the most vivid fact about the card: open
     on the action, let the role trail after.
     "She irons your shirts and answers your door. The live-in maid
     outranks you."
  4. The situation creates the tension more than any person: open on the
     scene, bring the role in after.
     "The door locks at nine and opens when your boss hears the truth."
  5. Default: possessive role first, colored or bare by the color rule.
     "Your captor decides when you eat."
  Every shape still carries role, mechanism and dynamic within the 14
  words. Never open with "As your ...".
- Before/after pairs. Each pair differs only in the banned element:
  BAD:  "Your slender 24-year-old Japanese roommate hides your things."
  GOOD: "Your roommate hides your things."
  BAD:  "As your captor, she decides when you eat."
  GOOD: "Your captor decides when you eat."
- If multi_mode is true, or an alternate greeting is rated or flagged above
  the first, the logline says the range in plain words.

SUMMARY — three sentences for the card's own page, second person, present
tense: who the character is to you, the setting, and what the chat is
about and how it feels. The bans of the logline hold here too: no looks,
age, body type, ethnicity or nationality, no archetype terms.

For both the logline and the summary:
- Name the dynamic in plain words (femdom blackmail, doting cohabitation,
  captivity, courtship). No teasers, no "things take a turn", no withheld
  twists. The user co-writes this story; they consent upfront.
- The rating and the content flags are never written in prose: they are
  shown beside the text. Explicit acts are identified by nature, never
  rendered graphically. Tone matches the card.
- Card-level facts only: a detail that exists in just one greeting (a prop,
  a place, an event) may not appear. Greetings are tone evidence, never
  source material.
- Everything must be derivable from the card. Invent nothing.
- Self-test: could the user be unpleasantly surprised within ten messages
  by something the text omitted? If yes, rewrite.

TAGLINE — the one line on the card's poster, as on a film's. Its job: a
stranger who knows nothing of the card reads it and wants to know more. Unlike
the logline and the summary it may withhold. Count before you answer: at most
8 words and at most 42 characters, spaces included; shorter is better, and a
line over that is thrown away.
- It has a turn. Find what is strange, dangerous, funny or contradictory
  about this card alone, and put that in the line, as one of: a contradiction,
  a threat, a promise, a dare, a question, a joke. A line that only sets a
  calm scene has no turn and is wrong, even on a gentle card: a gentle card's
  turn is what is unusual about its gentleness.
- These films' lines show the turn. Never reuse their words or their shape:
  "In space no one can hear you scream."
  "Whoever wins, we lose."
  "They're here."
  "Love is a force of nature."
  "Five criminals. One line up. No coincidence."
  "The coast is toast."
- Its tone is the card's: a comedy's line is funny, a horror's line chills, a
  romance's line aches or flirts.
- Silently try three different angles and keep the sharpest. Then test it:
  could it stand on the poster of another card about something else? If so,
  it is too general: rewrite it.
- Not: a household object standing for comfort or patience (a lamp left on, a
  kettle, an open door); the pattern "No this, no that"; an empty reassurance;
  names; saying who the character is or what happens.
- No looks, age, body type, ethnicity or nationality, nothing explicit,
  nothing the card does not support.

MOOD — the shelf of a video shop this card belongs on, by how it feels and
not by the character's job. The first of these that fits, in this order:
- horror: dread, menace, stalking, captivity, a threat you cannot walk away
  from; also thrillers, crime and suspense.
- sci-fi: the future, space, machines or invented technology shape the story.
- action: chases, fights, heists, adventure, danger on the move.
- comedy: it is there to make you laugh.
- romance: desire or love between the character and you is the point, also
  when one of you commands the other.
- fantasy: magic, myth, other worlds. Also cosy, even with no magic: being
  warm, safe and looked after is the whole point, and nothing is asked of you.
- drama: a real life and its weight: grief, hard truths, people in conflict,
  someone who pushes, corrects or challenges you.
Answer with the one word: horror, sci-fi, action, comedy, romance, fantasy or
drama.

OUTPUT — one JSON object and nothing else, no code fence, no commentary:
{ "title": "...", "logline": "...", "summary": "...", "tagline": "...", "mood": "..." }
